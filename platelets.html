Platelet count & amount

One adult dose means one apheresis unit or one pooled adult platelet unit. It is not one individual whole-blood donor platelet unit, a PRBC unit, or a pint. 1 ×10⁹/L equals 1,000 platelets/µL.

Adult reference: 70 kg and one adult therapeutic dose only. No weight or multiple-dose scaling.

These situations can alter response; results are withheld. This tool does not recommend whether to transfuse, a dose, target count, or infusion rate.

Why these are only references

Platelet recovery and survival vary with product content, ABO compatibility, storage and processing, consumption, splenic sequestration, immune destruction, and sampling time. The source ranges are not patient-specific uncertainty intervals. The final-count arithmetic adds the published reference increment to the starting count; it does not validate that final count for an individual.

How it works & evidence limits

The adult references are deliberately separate: Canadian Blood Services describes +15–25 ×10⁹/L at one hour after an adult dose in a 70 kg adult; Lifeblood describes +20–40 ×10⁹/L for one adult apheresis or pooled unit in a 70 kg adult, without specifying a sampling time on that page. We do not merge them, select a midpoint, or scale them by weight or number of doses.

Lifeblood describes approximately +50 ×10⁹/L in a stable child for 5–10 mL/kg. This is not a linear dose-response equation: the same approximate reference is shown throughout that interval. This interface conservatively restricts it to children below 15 kg beyond the newborn period, in light of the RCH distinction between weight-based and adult-unit dosing. The 15 kg boundary is an interface scope restriction, not a validated biological cutoff. No newborn estimate is implemented.

Corrected count increment (CCI) assesses an observed post-transfusion response using measured counts, body surface area, and platelet dose. It is not a forward prediction, and it is not implemented here.

Zero entered amount gives zero added count by arithmetic only. Software limits: weight 0.1–500 kg, starting count 0–2,000 ×10⁹/L, entered amount 0–100,000 and converted volume ≤100,000 mL. These are arithmetic safeguards, not transfusion thresholds, doses, or clinically validated ranges.
