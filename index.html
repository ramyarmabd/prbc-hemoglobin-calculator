SEPARATE APPROXIMATION

RCH pediatric approximation

For children under 20 kg beyond the newborn period, algebraically inverted from RCH’s volume formula: rise (g/dL) = transfused mL ÷ (5 × weight kg). This uses different assumptions from the mass-balance estimate and is not a correction to it.

≈

Read as arithmetic, not a measured outcome.

Assumes retention of transfused red cells and unchanged circulating blood volume after equilibration. Immediate volume expansion, fluid redistribution, sampling time, red-cell survival, and individual variability are not predicted.

BEHIND THE ESTIMATE

A transparent mass-balance model

The equations

  1. Blood volume (mL) = weight (kg) × estimated mL/kg, or the selected alternative.
  2. Delivered Hb (g) = PRBC volume (mL) × bag Hb (g) ÷ bag volume (mL).
  3. Hb rise (g/dL) = delivered Hb (g) ÷ [blood volume (mL) ÷ 100].
  4. Final Hb (g/dL) = starting Hb + calculated rise.

For multiple bags, this arithmetic assumes the same reference concentration for all transfused volume. It does not establish accuracy for multiple-unit transfusions.

What the adult research establishes

The 2019 donor–component–recipient study examined adult single-unit transfusion episodes. Its statistical regression is different from this mass-balance model; it does not validate pediatric or multiple-unit predictions.

The study observed a relationship between starting Hb and the increment. This mass model does not capture that relationship: starting Hb affects only the final sum. No research regression, invented coefficients, intercept, correction factors, or patient-specific uncertainty interval are implemented. Adding regression effects to this model could double-count overlapping effects.

Validation status & practical limitations

Software tests compare the production calculation module with independent arithmetic, conversions, adult blood-volume methods, RCH’s 10 kg/100 mL example, and NBA neonatal table values under the table’s own product assumptions. Agreement with arithmetic or published examples is not clinical validation.

Age defaults are adjustable assumptions: adult 70, older infant/child 70, term newborn 80, very preterm newborn 100 mL/kg. NBA Appendix G describes approximately 70 for older infants/children, 80–85 for term infants, and 100–120 for extremely preterm infants. These categories do not represent every infant, gestational age, or clinical state.

Actual PRBC volume and Hb concentration, recipient blood volume, red-cell retention, and sampling time may differ from the entered assumptions. This estimator has no prospective or retrospective clinical validation and no quantified patient-specific uncertainty. Clinical decisions require an appropriately qualified clinician and local policies.

All calculations stay in this browser. No patient identifiers, analytics, input submission, or persistent patient-data storage.
